Level 11: Teaching without a degree

Flo wants to teach but believes he must first earn a formal teaching degree.

Time allocation

You're facing a weekend packed with a friend visiting, a professional deadline, and a personal wellness goal. Choose which commitments get priority, what can be postponed, and how to keep the plan realistic.

The Assumption

You may be assuming all commitments are equally urgent.

The Basic Truth

Time is limited and energy is not. What matters most is not just the task, but whether it advances your real priorities or simply adds pressure.

The Tip

"List fixed commitments first, then compare the remaining options against their value and cost. Keep the plan small enough that it feels sustainable."

Difficult conversation

You need to tell someone close that their recent behavior is affecting you negatively. Decide how to frame the feedback, when to bring it up, and what outcome would feel respectful to both people.

The Assumption

You may be assuming they already understand how their actions affect you.

The Basic Truth

People rarely read minds. Clear, specific examples are more useful than general criticism.

The Tip

"Name the behavior, describe the impact, and offer a positive next step. Keep the focus on actions, not identity, so the conversation stays constructive."

Habit shift

You want to start a new daily habit, but previous attempts failed because they were too ambitious. Pick a small, concrete first action and identify the environmental trigger that will make it actually happen.

The Assumption

You may be assuming motivation alone will carry the habit forward.

The Basic Truth

Habits grow through consistency, not one-time effort. A tiny action linked to an existing routine is more reliable than a big goal.

The Tip

"Choose a habit small enough to do even when you don't feel like it, and attach it to something you already do every day."

Core value conflict

You face a choice where both paths align with deeply held values: pursuing an ambitious goal that would require abandoning time with family, or staying present with loved ones and letting the opportunity pass. Neither choice feels fully right. Define which value takes priority in this season of your life, and articulate why the other one doesn't disappear—it just takes a different form.

The Assumption

You may be assuming that one of your values is less important than you thought, or that a perfect choice exists that honors both fully.

The Basic Truth

The most meaningful choices are not between good and bad, but between two goods. Growth means consciously choosing which good takes priority right now, while acknowledging the other value remains real and will emerge again in different seasons.

The Tip

"Name the tradeoff clearly. Ask yourself: what season am I in, and which value needs to lead for now? Then commit to that choice without pretending the other value doesn't matter."

Flo's teaching path

Flo wants to teach but doesn't have a formal teaching degree. He believes the only valid path is a bachelor in education, even though some high-demand areas hire teachers with direct experience and fewer bureaucratic hurdles. Decide how to target those regions, gain experience, and treat the degree as optional rather than mandatory.

The Assumption

You assumed you absolutely need a teaching bachelor degree before you can start teaching.

The Basic Truth

Teaching in some areas is in high demand. High demand creates less bureaucracy. By targeting those regions directly, you can question the requirement of having a degree and focus on what actually matters: experience and budget.

The Tip

"Because I know [high-demand teaching areas relax degree requirements] is definitely true, my next step is to [build teaching experience through tutoring, volunteer work, or substitute teaching in those regions], rather than worrying about [needing a formal bachelor degree first]. I found out that my only hurdle is experience and a budget, not a degree."
