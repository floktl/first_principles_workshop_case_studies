Flo wants to teach but believes he must first earn a formal teaching degree.

Time allocation

You're facing a weekend packed with a friend visiting, a professional deadline, and a personal wellness goal. Choose which commitments get priority, what can be postponed, and how to keep the plan realistic.

1. DEFINE — What is the real goal?

The real goal is not to do everything. The real goal is to spend the weekend in a way that protects the most important commitments without creating unnecessary stress.

2. HUNT — What assumption is blocking the solution?

The hidden assumption is that all commitments are equally important and must all be completed perfectly. This creates pressure before any prioritization happens.

3. STRIP — What basic truths remain?

Time is limited. Energy is limited. Some commitments are fixed, while others are flexible. Not every task has the same consequence if it is delayed or reduced.

4. REBUILD — What better path follows from those truths?

First place the fixed commitments. Then compare the remaining tasks by value, urgency, and energy cost. The better plan is not the fullest plan, but the one that can actually be followed.

5. ACTION — What is the next 24-hour task?

In the next 24 hours, make a simple weekend schedule with fixed commitments first, then choose one professional priority and one wellness action that are realistic.

Difficult conversation

You need to tell someone close that their recent behavior is affecting you negatively. Decide how to frame the feedback, when to bring it up, and what outcome would feel respectful to both people.

1. DEFINE — What is the real goal?

The real goal is not to win an argument. The goal is to improve the relationship by making the problem visible in a respectful and specific way.

2. HUNT — What assumption is blocking the solution?

The hidden assumption is that the other person already understands how their behavior affects you. Another assumption may be that honesty must automatically create conflict.

3. STRIP — What basic truths remain?

People cannot respond to what they do not know. Specific examples are easier to understand than general blame. A calm time and a clear request make the conversation safer.

4. REBUILD — What better path follows from those truths?

The better approach is to describe the behavior, explain the impact, and ask for a concrete change. Focus on actions rather than attacking the person’s identity.

5. ACTION — What is the next 24-hour task?

In the next 24 hours, write a short conversation plan: one behavior, one impact, and one respectful request.

Habit shift

You want to start a new daily habit, but previous attempts failed because they were too ambitious. Pick a small, concrete first action and identify the environmental trigger that will make it actually happen.

1. DEFINE — What is the real goal?

The real goal is not to transform your whole life immediately. The goal is to create a habit that is small enough to repeat consistently.

2. HUNT — What assumption is blocking the solution?

The hidden assumption is that motivation will be enough. Another assumption is that a habit must be big to matter.

3. STRIP — What basic truths remain?

Motivation changes. Environment matters. Small actions are easier to repeat. A habit becomes stronger when it is attached to something you already do.

4. REBUILD — What better path follows from those truths?

Instead of designing the perfect routine, design the smallest version. Attach it to an existing trigger, such as brushing your teeth, making coffee, or closing your laptop.

5. ACTION — What is the next 24-hour task?

In the next 24 hours, choose one tiny habit and connect it to one existing daily routine. Make it so small that skipping it feels harder than doing it.

Core value conflict

You face a choice where both paths align with deeply held values: pursuing an ambitious goal that would require abandoning time with family, or staying present with loved ones and letting the opportunity pass. Neither choice feels fully right. Define which value takes priority in this season of your life, and articulate why the other one doesn't disappear—it just takes a different form.

1. DEFINE — What is the real goal?

The real goal is not to find a perfect option with no sacrifice. The goal is to make a conscious choice between two meaningful values.

2. HUNT — What assumption is blocking the solution?

The hidden assumption is that if one value leads right now, the other value has been betrayed. You may also be assuming that the “right” choice should feel painless.

3. STRIP — What basic truths remain?

Both values are real. Time and attention are limited. Different seasons of life require different priorities. Choosing one value now does not erase the other.

4. REBUILD — What better path follows from those truths?

The better path is to name the tradeoff honestly and decide which value needs to lead in this season. Then find a smaller way to still honor the other value.

5. ACTION — What is the next 24-hour task?

In the next 24 hours, write down the two values in conflict, choose which one leads this season, and define one small way to respect the other value.

Flo's teaching path

Flo wants to teach but doesn't have a formal teaching degree. He believes the only valid path is a bachelor in education, even though some high-demand areas hire teachers with direct experience and fewer bureaucratic hurdles. Decide how to target those regions, gain experience, and treat the degree as optional rather than mandatory.

1. DEFINE — What is the real goal?

The real goal is not “get a bachelor degree.” The real goal is: start teaching in a realistic way. The degree is only one possible path, not the goal itself.

2. HUNT — What assumption is blocking the solution?

The hidden assumption is: “I cannot teach unless I first complete a formal teaching degree.” This assumption makes the path feel expensive, slow, and almost impossible before any real teaching experience begins.

3. STRIP — What basic truths remain?

Some regions or teaching areas have high demand. High demand often lowers barriers. Teaching ability can also be demonstrated through experience, tutoring, volunteering, substitute teaching, workshops, mentoring, or subject expertise. So the basic truth is: the first hurdle is not necessarily a degree; it may be experience, proof of ability, and finding the right region.

4. REBUILD — What better path follows from those truths?

Instead of starting with the most expensive and formal route, rebuild the path from the goal: find places where teachers are needed, check their actual requirements, and build direct teaching experience first. The new logic is: target high-demand regions, gain practical experience, and treat the degree as optional unless it is truly required.

5. ACTION — What is the next 24-hour task?

In the next 24 hours, Flo should research 5 high-demand teaching regions or programs, write down their actual entry requirements, and contact at least 2 schools, tutoring centers, or education programs to ask what experience would make him eligible.
